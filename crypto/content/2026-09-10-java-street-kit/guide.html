Gatekeeper on the street

The guide for the day

Version 10 Sep 2026
Read it twice

You are getting people to play a 60-second phone game called Gatekeeper and to put their score on its leaderboard. The game lives on an independent site about Sable Network, an AI compute service. The site is run by a private person who holds the SABL token and is not part of Sable Network.

You are not selling anything. You are not promoting a token. You are running a game day, and the count of played runs is how the day is measured.

01The one rule

No token. No price. No "invest", "buy", "early", "moon", "SABL".

Not on the poster, not from your mouth, not in your posts.

Why. Indonesia's regulator OJK only allows licensed exchanges to advertise crypto assets, it summoned influencers in June 2026 for promoting unlicensed platforms, and its 2026 conduct rule (POJK 6/2026) requires paid promotion to be disclosed and forbids promises of returns. SABL is not on the OJK list. If you promote it on the street, the risk is yours, not the founder's. The game is legal and fun. Keep it there.

If someone asks "is this crypto?"
"The site is about an AI service that also has a token. I am not here for the token and I am not allowed to promote it. The game is free: no wallet, no account, no money."
If someone asks "can I earn money?"
"No. It is a game. The best score gets its name lit on the board, that is all."

02The link and the QR

sable.primecircle.cloud/java
That short link sends the phone to the game with the tag "java" attached; the QR carries the same destination in full (sable.primecircle.cloud/?src=java#play). Every run that starts from either is counted under "java" on the board. That count is how the founder measures your day. Use only these: on the poster, in the QR, and when you type it for someone. The plain site link without the tag counts nothing.

The site loads a small 3D library, so it needs a normal phone browser and mobile data. It works on Android and iPhone. If a phone is very old, use the Demo.

03The game, so you can explain it

You are the door of an AI agent. Requests arrive at the door. Sealed ones pass and become receipts. Broken seals and runaway loops must be refused before they reach the door. Only refusals build your streak. A wave held without a leak gives budget back. There is no clock: the run lasts as long as your budget. Runs of 20 seconds or longer are ranked. The board replays every run from its taps before it counts, so a faked score never lands.

The Demo button plays a 60-second run by itself. Use it to show a group what the game looks like before they try.

04The 20-second pitch

EnglishWant a 60-second game? You are the gatekeeper for an AI. Scan, play, get on the board. Free, no account.

Then hand them the QR, let them press Play, and step back. When the run ends, ask them to type a name and, if they have one, their X handle, and press submit. That is one counted run. Names can be anything; handles are optional.

05Ask for a video

If they liked it, ask them to film their next run (10 to 60 seconds, phone held upright) and post it on X with #GatekeeperJava and @Sablenetwork. Everyone who posts one within the week is in a random draw for a prize, drawn and announced by the site's owner on his X account one week after the day. The leaderboard is the prize for playing; the draw is for the videos.

The short version to say out loudFilm your run, post it with #GatekeeperJava and @Sablenetwork, and you are in the draw.

Two things to keep straight: the video is about the game, not the token (a video that mentions the token is out of the draw, for the same reason as the one rule), and filming a friend needs their yes.

06Where to go

Places where people have five minutes and a phone: computer science faculties, coworking spaces, tech meetups, cafes near a university, game cafes, hackathon venues. Not traffic lights, not places of worship, not schools with minors.

Ask the owner before you put up a poster inside. For posters outside, check your city's rules; many cities fine wild posting. A hand-held sign is always fine.

07What counts

  • Counted: a run started from your link, 20 seconds or longer, submitted with a name. Runs the referee flags as scripted do not count.
  • See the count yourself, any time: sable.primecircle.cloud/api/game/src?tag=java (runs, players, best). On the game page, a tab "from java" lists the runs.
  • Photos are the story. Runs are the measure.

08Photos and content

  • Ask before you photograph anyone: "Boleh saya foto untuk media sosial?" No photos of children. No faces of people who said no.
  • Send the same evening: 10 to 20 photos, a few short vertical clips of 10 to 20 seconds, the city and the place, and how many people played. Send by WhatsApp or Telegram.
  • If you post about it yourself: no token, no price, tag only the site. Say who you ran it with, so nobody thinks you are Sable. A caption is in appendix B. If anything about this day is sponsored, Indonesian rules (POJK 6/2026) require you to say so in your own post.

09Do not

  • Do not shout anything about SABL or crypto. Shouting "come play a game" is fine.
  • No stickers on public property, no posters over other people's posters, no blocking a sidewalk.
  • Do not collect anyone's phone number, wallet address, or email. The game needs none of them.
  • Do not promise anything beyond the board and the video draw. The draw is one week after the day; what the prize is, is the site owner's to announce.
  • Do not argue with police or security. Pack up and move.

10Day checklist

  • Phone charged, power bank, mobile data.
  • Morning: open the link on your own phone, play one run, submit it, and check that it shows on the "from java" tab. If it does not, message the founder before you start.
  • Posters and tape (with permission), one hand-held sign, the flyers. Scan one printed poster with your own phone before you leave: it must open the game with "from java" on the board.
  • Say the one rule out loud to your friends before you start.
  • Evening: photos, clips, the count, sent.
