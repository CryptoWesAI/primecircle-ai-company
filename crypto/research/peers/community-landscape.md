# How Small "Confidential/Verifiable AI Compute" Crypto Projects Grew Communities, 2023-2026

Compiled 2026-09-09, for an outsider holder running a neutral observatory site, not a team member.

## Channels

Across Akash, Phala, io.net, and Render, the pattern holds: X is where a project is discovered and announcements land first; Telegram is fast informal discussion; Discord is deeper, structured engagement; GitHub is where builders verify a project is real; Farcaster is smaller, mainly onchain-native audiences and grant scouting (e.g., Base's Farcaster-sourced builder search). Typical path: X to Telegram to Discord, GitHub as the credibility check underneath (aggregated industry commentary, not one primary study; a pattern, not a statistic).

## What moved people

**Output-tied ambassador and grant programs.** Phala's Ambassador Program pays PHA to a small ranked cohort (Oct 2025: 1 Head, 2 Senior, 10 Active, sharing 45,000 PHA/month) for marketing and content, per Phala's own pages. Virtuals' "Revenue Incentives for AI" tied builder payouts to agents that actually earned revenue; Epoch 1 reportedly paid ~$500,000 across 1,500+ revenue-generating agents in two weeks (@virtuals_io, undated). The signal: pay for verified output, not follower counts.

**Hackathons and incentivized testnets with real prize pools.** Akash ran a GPU-marketplace incentivized testnet June 20-July 11, 2023 (Akash GitHub), then a third testnet with $200,000 in developer incentives (PR Newswire). Phala co-hosted a $100,000 Brussels AI hackathon in summer 2024 and showed up at EthDenver, Token2049, and Devcon that year (Phala's 2024 year-in-review). These work because they produce something checkable: a shipped integration, a testnet transaction, a PR.

**Independent dashboards, explorers, and comparison content**, the category an observatory sits in. They work because a newcomer can verify a claim themselves, and a neutral third party's honest comparison reads as more credible than the project's own marketing.

## What backfired

Fake Discord/Telegram membership and bot engagement are now easy to detect; a padded member count mismatched to real chat activity is itself a red flag. Undisclosed paid shilling produced its own backlash: ZachXBT's exposure of undisclosed influencer payments ($50-$60,000 per post, per reporting) found fewer than 5 of 200 examined accounts disclosed the arrangement, damaging every project connected. Raid culture (coordinated reply-spam) reads as manufactured to attentive observers and costs more credibility than it buys. Throughline: any tactic manufacturing a number (members, engagement, sentiment) rather than a real artifact (a transaction, a PR, a working dashboard) is now cheap to expose.

## Regulatory note

MiCA applies to the founder as an EU-based holder of a token (SABL) discussed publicly: marketing communications about a crypto-asset must be fair, clear, not misleading, and any promotional or recommendation content must disclose the discloser's conflict of interest, i.e. that they hold it (EUR-Lex MiCA summary; Sumsub, InnReg guides). In practice: every public post, page, or dashboard touching SABL needs a consistent one-line holder disclosure.

## Ranked tactics for an observatory with a leaderboard game, effort to effect

1. Persistent, honest "I hold SABL" disclosure across the site and every post mentioning it: minutes of effort, non-negotiable under MiCA and for credibility.
2. Publish the leaderboard's scoring logic and data source openly so a skeptic can verify it isn't gamed: low effort, high trust payoff.
3. Write one honest, sourced comparison against a larger neighbor, strengths and weaknesses both ways: a few hours, strong credibility with the audience being courted.
4. Cross-post the observatory's real on-chain findings (burns, receipts, spend caps) into the project's own Telegram/Discord as a member, not an announcement: low effort, steady trust-building.
5. Open-source the dashboard's code on GitHub: moderate effort, turns the site itself into a checkable artifact.
6. Fund a small, output-tied leaderboard prize personally, disclosed as personal: moderate cost, mirrors what worked for Phala/Virtuals at far smaller scale.
7. Show up, in person or async, at one AI-agent or DePIN-adjacent hackathon and write up what was learned: moderate effort, real relationship-building.
8. Pitch the observatory as a case study to a crypto-data newsletter or podcast once it has a track record: moderate effort, depends on having something worth covering.
9. Contribute a small, real GitHub PR or issue to the project itself: moderate effort, the highest-credibility "supporter" signal, since it's unfakeable.
10. Never run or fund a raid, reply-spam campaign, or purchased engagement of any kind: zero effort to abstain, purely defensive but non-negotiable.
